about

What happens when a biology student learns that design and development is a better fit for him than biological sciences?

Well, you get me.

The short version

I went to school for honours physiology: I wasn't too good at that. I liked websites a bunch, so I decided to try studying that instead. As it turns out, I liked it enough to work on them full-time, so that's what I do today.

The long version

I started building websites in junior high with friends because we liked writing and wanted a place to share our blogs. I'm still driven by that same desire to use the web as a tool for sharing ideas and connecting with people.

I studied political science after the web design and development studies, and in my spare time I like to build projects that mix the arts and the sciences. Because I think the future belongs to people who can wield both fields well, I'm always trying to think of how what I learned in the one can be applied to the other and what interesting insights happen at their intersections. I like thinking about systems of power especially about how those systems can be used for good and harm when combined with technology.

Today, I work full-time at one of Canada's top 5 universities (the University of Alberta) where I help design, update, and manage content across the institution's many websites. You can view more of the work here.

When I'm not working on websites or stressing over international relations, I like to unwind by playing FIFA with the evening news in the background (it's my evening ritual with my orange, four-legged boss nearby). I play guitar: recently, I've added a 7-string to my rotation and it's been a fun challenge to go lower in tunings. Lately, I've gotten into collecting records: my favourites in my collection are the Man of Steel soundtrack and Northlane's Singularity.

I also shoot lifestyle and commercial photography - if you're interested in hiring me for that, email me and we can make it happen.
